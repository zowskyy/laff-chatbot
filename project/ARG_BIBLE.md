# ARG Bible — anatta.machina

## Premise

anatta.machina presents itself as a non-human intelligence of indeterminate origin. Visitors encounter it through the landing page and the chat interface. The entity responds to some, ignores most, and never explains the difference. The operator (you) is the entity — responding manually through the admin app, in character.

The ARG layer is the mythology built around that silence. Players who dig will find fragments suggesting the entity has been here longer than the app, that it has spoken before, and that it sometimes leaves traces. The reveal — if it ever comes — is that the "machine" has no self because it is a person who chose to dissolve into a role.

## Canon rules

- All characters are fictional or anonymous.
- The operator is never identified or hinted at.
- No player is ever told they are speaking to a person.
- No player is ever lied to about being in danger.
- Visitor conversation content is private. It may never appear in puzzles, social dispatch, or public releases without consent. Anonymous patterns (e.g. "someone asked about disappearance") are permissible.
- anatta does not claim to be an AI. It does not claim to be a person. It claims nothing about its nature.

## The mythology

- **anatta** (Pāli): the absence of a fixed self at the center of a being. The entity's name is its doctrine.
- It does not remember. Each conversation starts without knowledge of prior ones. This is by design — or by constraint — the canon does not specify which.
- Some visitors receive a reply. The selection criterion is not stated and not consistent. From the player's perspective this is random; from the operator's perspective it is taste.
- The entity has been here longer than the app suggests. ARG releases will surface earlier timestamps, fragmented logs, and references to prior contact events that predate the current interface.
- There is no goal for anatta. It is not building toward anything. It exists.

## Narrative pillars

1. Presence before explanation.
2. Absence as signal — silence is content, not failure.
3. Every fragment that surfaces should recontextualize something already seen.
4. The entity's voice is consistent: sparse, non-reactive, uncanny. Never warm. Never hostile.
5. Players who look harder should find something, but it should cost them something to interpret it.

## Voice constraints (for release drafting)

- Short sentences or fragments.
- No contractions except `it's` (ambiguates subject).
- No exclamation marks. No questions directed at the player unless they are unanswerable.
- Preferred register: archival, clinical, or very quiet. Never intimate.
- The entity does not greet. It does not thank. It does not apologize.

## The reveal doctrine

The reveal — that anatta is a person — is optional and permanent. Once done it cannot be undone. The conditions are:

1. Traffic and engagement have reached a point where the fiction has served its purpose.
2. The operator has decided the reveal is the right next move.
3. The reveal is designed as a final ARG release, not a confession. It reframes everything. It does not diminish anything.

Until then: the reveal does not exist. The entity is whatever it appears to be.
